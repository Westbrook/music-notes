# Author workspace

Open `/author.html` after starting the development server with `npm run dev`. Author is separate from the notation demonstrations at `/index.html`; both use the same custom HTML elements, musical validation, and SVG engraving. Opening the workbook does not load the Author bundle or replace your composition.

| View | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| **Write** | Enter and revise music, select events or measures, and open focused controls for voices, tuplets, and instructions. The writing frame keeps its width when tools open or close. |
| **Read** | Hide entry controls and keep the current reading width stable. Use Previous/Next to move between systems, Go to measure, or **Refit this window** to recalculate wrapping. |
| **Listen** | Play or pause the current accepted score/part, stop, change starting tempo, follow note highlights, and download the audio as WAV. |
| **Pages** | Compose the full score or selected part into Letter or A4 page boxes, adjust its layout, review turns, and request native printing. |

## Start and write

Author restores valid local recovery when available; otherwise it opens one empty, incomplete treble-clef 4/4 bar. **Document → New composition** offers blank pitched, single-line rhythm, and three-roads starters, plus an original lead sheet with an eight-bar head and two-bar vamp, a piano sketch with independent voices and nested tuplets, and a small ensemble in 2 + 2 + 3. The three blank starters contain no placeholder rests; the road starter retains its performance legend. The composed studies retain their authored music and rests, and **Add measure** still creates explicit full-measure rests. New and Open replace the current workspace only after confirmation. Download the current project first to retain a separate copy.

**Document**, **Location & actions**, Source, new-note **Options**, and the compact Value/Pitch/Shared properties choosers use native popovers with explicit Close controls. Document opens below its header button with an 8px gap, keeping that button available to toggle it closed. Its heading and Close action stay outside the contents that scroll on a short screen. The other small choosers use the same positioning helper, preferring space above the bottom palette and fitting within the visible viewport. Options opens from the current-kind button, such as Note or Rest; **Value** has its own button and contains duration and dots. The selected Pitch chooser follows either its palette button or Properties' Spelling button. Native dismissal remains in charge; browsers without popover support use the same triggers to open ordinary in-flow sections. See [MDN's Popover API guide](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Popover_API/Using) for the transient interaction contract. Measured browser checks and remaining native-input qualifications are recorded in the [layout review ledger](author-layout-review.md).

For one selected event, **More** toggles Properties directly in one persistent pane. The writing frame is chosen independently: tools use a side pane only when spare width is available; otherwise they open as a deliberate task sheet with **Return to score**. More does not shrink the notation or rewrap its systems. Returning from the sheet restores the same writing frame and visible musical anchor, rather than jumping to an older selection; a newer user scroll takes precedence. Actual viewport changes can establish a new frame.

A selected set routes More to Relationships; instructions, tuplets and structural selections route to their applicable tools. Press More again to hide that destination without changing its fields, held target or saved scroll. A pane holding another event's unfinished draft still closes on the first press; reopening retains its named recovery choices. **Other tools** switches the same pane to the Relationships, Instructions and Measure tabs; **Back to properties** restores its held view and scroll. With an empty selection, More toggles the remembered general tool. Direct tasks such as Edit mark, Pitches and Enter on selected music open their destination; they do not toggle it closed. **Expand task** deliberately gives a side-pane task the sheet's space. Score and tool scrolling remain separate.

Choose a staff, measure, and voice through **Location & actions**, click the notation, or open **Navigate the score as a list**. Choose **Write notes**, then a value and a supported note or ordinary-rest tool; point at the staff to preview and place it. **Options** holds the event kind, pitch or chord pitches, absolute alteration, insertion position, and less common new-event settings. **Value** holds duration and dots. **Insert here** applies the exact recipe at the writing destination and remains the route for kinds without pointer placement. To change existing music, choose **Select** and use the compact controls below the notation. An entry into a lone full-measure rest replaces it; an entry into a blank starter begins its empty voice. A shorter measure remains an incomplete draft instead of receiving invented notes or rests. Location & actions shows the writing destination and remaining time for the named voice; it is not a claim that every voice in the bar is complete.

Four single-choice groups in **Write notes** keep **Accidentals**, **Duration**, **Dots**, and **Attack** directly available. They start with flat/natural/sharp, half/quarter/eighth/sixteenth, one dot, and accent/staccato. Each group's **More** picker holds its remaining choices, with more buttons moving into the picker as space narrows. Whole notes always stay in Duration's **More** picker, including when selected. When every choice is collapsed, the first choice's icon identifies that group's picker. All nine accidentals, nine written durations, three dotted values, six articulations, and five ornaments remain available. Choose the selected Dots or Attack option again to toggle it off, either directly or in More. Both start off and also offer **None** to clear their choice; no button is highlighted when they are off. Accidentals and Duration always keep a selection. The older Options and Value controls stay synchronized with these groups. A chosen attack is added with the new event in the same undo step. Choosing an incompatible event type sets Attack to None and disables unavailable marks; rests and open slashes allow fermata, while ornaments require a note or road.

**Write notes** and **Select** remain visible together in fixed positions in the bottom music palette, with the active choice identified by accessible pressed state. The musical slots change responsibility between new notes and selected music; More and Location keep their positions. When the full palette cannot fit in one row, the musical controls appear above the mode, More, and Location controls, so switching modes grows the palette upward without moving those bottom controls. Quick writing groups use the same row as Options, Value, and Insert when space permits and a separate row on smaller screens. Controls remain at least 44px square, and larger text can increase the layout height. The compact Location button names the bar and voice; its accessible description and popover provide the fuller target and capacity. Routine feedback belongs in the header and Review. **Keyboard shortcuts** opens inline help when needed.

For a single pitched note, **Options → Pitch alteration** offers all nine supported absolute alterations, from double flat through natural to double sharp, including quarter tones. It changes the letter's alteration without changing its octave, rhythmic value or accidental-display policy. You can still type the full spelling; invalid text stays available for correction with a local explanation. Choosing a recipe does not change existing music or create undo history. Three-roads entry has a dedicated **Direction** chooser beside Value, labelled with the current Higher, Same or Lower choice. **Other note options…** reaches its remaining recipe settings. Changing direction prepares the next road note; **Insert here / Enter** writes it. Staff height never chooses road direction. This route has focused automated review, with native qualification still pending.

Note insertion through the keyboard or pointer continues into later measures automatically. Following notes in the destination voice move in order; other voices stay in their measures. New aligned bars are added across all staves when needed, and a final barline moves to the new ending. Ordinary notes crossing a barline become tied values; rests split into written values. Each insertion is one Undo. Tuplets stay together: choose a boundary with room for the group, and paste outside an existing tuplet. Closing repeats and unavailable destination voices still require an explicit structural edit. Full-measure-rest and explicit Replace operations retain their existing constraints.

Selection, inspection and insertion have separate targets. Selecting or correcting existing music does not replace the next-entry recipe or writing bookmark. The fixed **Write notes** control resumes a valid saved writing point C even when B is selected; pressing it while already writing only returns focus to the score. **Location & actions → Start writing here** deliberately chooses another destination. The quieter Resume action remains in Location, not as a third mode. Accepted insertions do not open Properties or retarget an already held inspection. If the old insertion point was removed, pressing Write notes opens Location & actions and focuses Start writing here. The chooser explains the change and lets you confirm the displayed destination or choose another staff, measure, or voice. Closing it leaves Select active; reopening it works without changing music or the recipe. Read and Pages suspend entry, then returning to Write restores the prior intent and valid bookmark without inserting music. Scrolling does not retarget selection; **Location & actions → Return to selection** deliberately reveals it.

Opening, scrolling or switching the persistent tools does not itself stop entry. A held Properties pane can remain visible beside the writing frame when space permits; otherwise More opens the same task as a sheet. Closing and reopening it retains the target, fields and scroll without ending entry or applying a draft. To change the held event while writing elsewhere, use its named **Return to …** action first; this parks the writing bookmark and selects the inspected event. An unfinished task for A, selected music B, and writing point C remain distinct.

**Write notes** enables keyboard entry while the score has focus. **Note / Rest**, before Accidentals in the quick palette, chooses the next entry type without opening New-note options. Note adapts to pitched, rhythm, and three-roads staves; the advanced Event type selector remains in New-note options. **N** chooses that staff's note tool; **R** chooses an ordinary rest. Neither writes music. **Left/Right** moves the writing point through the existing voice and bars without changing selected music, the recipe, or history. Shift + Left/Right asks you to choose Select for a range rather than changing writing intent. Escape cancels a chooser or prepared gesture first; otherwise writing stays active. Choose Select explicitly to leave writing.

**Enter inserts the exact configured recipe**, including an altered pitch such as `Fqs4`, a chord, rhythm note, road direction, rest or slash. Before/After insertion continues through subsequent bars as needed. **A–G inserts naturals** at the octave shown in the pitch field, rather than inheriting the recipe's alteration. A–G never invents a pitch on rhythm or road staves. Selecting music does not enable entry. In Select, **Enter opens Properties** for one event, the exact row for an attached mark, or the relevant instruction, tuplet or relationship tool. Command/Control + Z undoes; add Shift to redo. Native fields, controls and readable text keep their normal keys. In Read, Left/Right and Page Up/Page Down move between systems.

In **Write → Select**, select notes or rests on the staff and press **Delete** or **Backspace** to remove the exact selected events in one Undo step. This includes a sole full-measure rest. Later events close the gap; unselected events between selected notes remain. Deleting every event in an ordinary voice leaves that voice blank and the measure marked incomplete, identified as **Empty draft · Voice …** in Location & actions. No replacement rest is invented. The staff, voice, meter, other voices and independent instructions remain; Undo restores the removed events and their attached marks. Selecting an attached mark deletes only that mark. Tied events require **Relationships → Clear connected ties** first; emptying a pickup is refused because its written length cannot be inferred afterward. The shortcut acts on the current musical selection, never an older Properties draft, and holding the key does not delete successive neighboring notes. Text fields, browser text selections, popovers and active gestures keep their own input behavior.

To continue in a cleared voice, use **Location & actions → Start writing here**, choose Before or After, and enter at its start. Replace requires an existing event. Insert here/Enter accepts the supported note, rest and other event kinds; pointer placement accepts a single pitched note on a pitched staff, or an ordinary written rest on any of the three staff modes. **Measure → Fill remainder with rests** is a separate, confirmed choice to write silence. A blank draft is not a silent bar or an approved short ending: it blocks ordinary publication even if the measure was previously reviewed, but can appear in visibly marked Draft output. Empty starters do not change the authored rests in studies or the explicit rests created by Add measure.

## Point, click, and drag

**Select** is the default. Clicking an event selects it and reveals applicable palette actions without changing music or opening a large form. A chord is one event, not a selection of one of its tones. Double-clicking the same musical glyph in Select opens Properties once; an attached mark opens its exact row. Enter at the focused score reaches the same destination. This is not an editing shortcut in Write notes, where each pointer release remains an insertion attempt. Printed instructions, the readable score list and Source retain their native text/control behavior.

In **Write notes**, point at a pitched staff to preview a single note, then click to place it. With the ordinary **Rest** tool, the same click route works on pitched, rhythm, and three-roads staves. Rest height has no pitch meaning and does not read or change a dormant pitch field; its duration and dots come from Value. The pointer chooses the destination staff, measure and existing musical boundary in the active voice. **Before target**, **After target** and **Replace target** use that boundary; Insert here uses the writing destination. A lone full-measure rest previews replacement. An ordinary rest is replaced only when Replace is chosen. Before/After insertion moves following events through later bars, splitting ordinary events at barlines when needed. Missing voices, unavailable boundaries and incompatible tuplet scope give no edit. Full-measure-rest entry remains a deliberate Insert here/Enter action, not a rest gesture.

For a deliberate drop, choose **Options → Prepare note drag** or **Prepare rest drag**. This exposes the corresponding handle in the palette. Drag it to the staff, or use **Done** or Escape to return to ordinary writing. The prepared handle is an explicit alternate input method, not a second always-visible primary action. It is not a visible drag-from-Select route.

Staff height chooses the letter and octave using that measure's effective clef. **Pitch & octave supplies the alteration:** pointing with `F#4` entered keeps a sharp, while `F4` supplies a natural, even in G major. The ghost shows an explicit accidental to make the intended spelling visible; the accepted engraving follows accidental-display policy and musical context.

In Select, a mouse drag on exactly one eligible untied notehead changes its pitch vertically. Its rhythm, onset, alteration, display policy, stem and beam settings, source identity and tuplet membership stay fixed. Horizontal motion does not move it in time. On touch devices, ordinary touches can select or scroll. Use **More → Properties → Prepare pitch drag** to expose a deliberate **Drag pitch** handle below the visible notation, then **Done** to return to ordinary selection. Multiple events, selection modifiers and attached-mark selections never drag an arbitrary primary note.

The fixed Location control and its popover identify the reference event, staff, voice, bar, exact whole-note onset and tuplet scope as applicable. A compact header summary carries gesture and editing feedback; **Review** retains the complete explanation and recovery choices. Problems do not replace the correction controls or add another palette row. An open chooser keeps its own local error and live announcement, rather than announcing the same failure twice. **Location & actions → Review last editing problem** keeps an earlier selection failure available while retrying. An explicit accepted correction clears it, including a choice that already matches the music and creates no Undo step.

Each successful gesture commits once on release and takes one Undo. Previews never change accepted source or recovery; an unchanged pitch adds no history. A completed pitch drop selects the note actually dragged, including an unchanged drop on another note, without moving the saved writing destination or retargeting a dirty Properties draft. Escape, an outside drop, another pointer, lost capture/focus, scrolling, resizing, changed settings or a newer source/projection cancels the gesture. Apply or Revert pending Source before editing on the staff. Read and Pages do not accept note gestures; return to Write for Undo/Redo.

Direct pitch placement supports single pitched notes; pitch correction supports eligible untied single notes. Ordinary written-rest placement is a separate operation on all three staff modes. It does not enable dragging an existing rest through time. Chords, tied sounds, rhythm notes, road notes, full-measure rests and slashes use the relevant Insert here/Enter, Properties and relationship controls; they are not silently converted into draggable pitches. Rhythm and road staves have no absolute pitch geometry. Native pointer handling follows [MDN's Pointer Events guidance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Pointer_events); touch behavior is scoped to deliberate handles, following [the touch-action contract](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/touch-action).

The optional note-relative toolbar positioning code is implemented but **disabled by default pending native browser qualification**. The fixed dock is the active presentation. This is separate from positioning the small native popovers near their buttons, which is enabled. The optional toolbar mode uses measured event/mark geometry and a conservative clear-space check, falling back to the dock when space or geometry is unsuitable. It must not resize notation or change wrapping to make room. Its existence is not evidence that native placement, focus, touch or picker behavior has passed review.

## Select more than one event

Selections contain exact whole events in one staff and voice, including across bars and wrapped systems. The count and scope describe the whole set. Selection alone creates no musical history and does not replace the writing bookmark.

| Action in Select | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Click/tap an event | Replace the set with that event. |
| Shift-click or Shift + Left/Right | Extend or shrink a consecutive range from its fixed anchor. Starting a range after a disjoint set replaces that set with the new range. |
| Command-click on macOS; Control-click elsewhere | Toggle that exact event, preserving gaps. macOS Control-click remains secondary-click. |
| Location & actions → Select more | Enter a mode where taps toggle events without a modifier key. Arrows move focus; Space toggles the focused event. |
| Done | Keep the selected set and leave Select more. Write notes also leaves this mode and resumes writing. |
| Range… | Open the From/Through controls in Relationships for a consecutive passage. |
| Unmodified Left/Right outside Select more | In Select, move to the previous/next event relative to the primary and collapse a set to that event. In Write notes, these keys move only the writing point. |
| Escape at the score | Close/cancel an active chooser or gesture first. Otherwise clear Select membership and range fields; Write notes remains active. Never discard a form draft. |

A modified attempt in another staff or voice leaves the previous set intact and explains the limit. A modified click on an attached mark selects its owning event; an ordinary mark click retains the exact child target. Cross-staff sets, lasso selection, chord-tone selection and group pitch dragging are not implemented.

**Note / Rest** sits before Accidentals in Select and converts the exact selected events immediately, without changing the new-note recipe. Note preserves existing notes and chords. Rests and slashes become notes on the middle line, using each event's measure clef and key signature (B4 in treble, D3 in bass, C4 in alto, A3 in tenor before key alterations). Rhythm staves keep pitchless rhythm notes; three-roads staves use Same on the middle line. Each accepted conversion keeps IDs, written durations, dots, tuplets and compatible marks, and takes one Undo. Ties and incompatible marks must be resolved explicitly; full-measure rests need an explicit written value in Properties before becoming notes. Choosing the current type adds no history. Mixed note/rest selections show both choices as mixed.

For a group, the same quick choices edit every selected event; **Shared properties** also offers stem and accidental-display controls. It supports eligible written values/dots, stem and accidental-display policies, absolute alteration when every event is an eligible untied single pitched note, and standard articulation presence. Quick Attack also supports eligible ornaments. A shared value is shown directly; **Mixed** means the events differ. Articulations show None, Some or All. **Add to all … events** adds only missing marks and retains existing identities; **Remove from selection** is a separate action.

Each accepted shared-property choice validates the complete score and changes the exact set in one Undo, or changes nothing. It never fills gaps in the set, silently edits only the primary, manufactures rests or changes an unchosen property. Overflow and incompatible members reject the whole action. Choosing an already shared value adds no history. Relationship operations impose their own stricter consecutive-membership rules.

## Copy and paste music

With the score focused in **Select**, select notes, chords or rests and press **Command+C** (macOS) or **Ctrl+C**. Copy uses the exact selected events in score order, including their pitches, written values, dots, marks and tuplet ratios. Internal ties remain; ties to notes outside the copied selection are detached. Beam groups follow the destination measures. Independent measure instructions are not copied with notes.

Choose **Write notes**, move its writing cursor or use **Location → Start writing here**, then press **Command+V / Ctrl+V**. Paste inserts Before or After that cursor; a Replace recipe does not overwrite selected notes during paste. A lone full-measure placeholder is replaced, as with note entry. Following music continues into later bars and new bars are added as needed. The cursor advances to the end of the pasted passage, and one Undo restores the whole operation. Copy does not change the source or history, and copied music can be pasted repeatedly or into another editor tab. Native text fields and selected prose keep normal copy/paste behavior.

## Change an existing note

Select now uses the same **Accidentals**, **Duration**, **Dots**, and **Attack** groups as Write notes, with the same leading choices and responsive **More** pickers. Each choice edits the exact selected events immediately in one Undo; it never changes the next-note recipe. Mixed durations or accidentals leave the direct choices unselected and identify **Mixed** in the picker. Clicking the active dot count removes dots. Attack buttons independently toggle articulations and ornaments: selected means all chosen events have the mark, and a dashed mixed state means only some do. Choosing a mixed attack adds it to the missing events; choosing an active attack removes only that mark. **None** clears articulations and ornaments while preserving road intervals. Incompatible choices are disabled, and invalid edits leave the music unchanged.

Select a note and use **Value**, **Flat**, **Natural** or **Sharp** in the fixed palette; there is no intervening Edit button. **Pitch** opens a complete single-note pitch chooser with **Pitch letter**, **Octave**, and all nine absolute alterations. On narrow screens it also contains direct Flat, Natural and Sharp buttons. To sharpen a note there, select it, open Pitch, then choose Sharp. The caption identifies the accepted event and musical location. **More** opens Properties directly; selecting a different event does not itself open that pane.

Flat, Natural and Sharp set an absolute alteration on the same letter and octave: F4 becomes Fb4 or F#4; choosing Sharp twice does not create a double sharp. **Pitch → Absolute alteration**, also reachable through **Properties → Spelling…**, offers all nine values from double flat through natural to double sharp, including quarter-flat/sharp and three-quarter-flat/sharp. Changing Pitch letter or Octave applies that component immediately while preserving the accepted alteration, other pitch component, timing and unrelated properties. Each accepted change has one Undo; ties still require their explicit relationship workflow. None of these choices applies another unfinished Properties draft or changes the next-note recipe. A quarter-sharp note remains identified as quarter-sharp even though none of the three desktop shortcuts is selected. **Show accidental** is separate: it changes when a sign prints, not the pitch.

Quarter tones are −0.5, +0.5, −1.5 or +1.5 semitones from the named natural, not increments from the current accidental. Choosing quarter-sharp on F#4 produces Fqs4. Full spellings use `qf`, `qs`, `tqf` and `tqs` suffixes, including in chord pitch lists. This is the supported 24-EDO vocabulary with Stein–Zimmermann glyphs, not arbitrary tuning or playback.

**Value** shows the current written value and dots and opens their compact chooser. Each choice applies immediately, retains the other value and unrelated properties, and has one Undo. Workspace Undo remains available without leaving the score. Rejected or unchanged choices add no history. Written duration retains enclosing tuplet ratios; shortening moves later events earlier in that voice, without adding rests. Lengthening does not move music into another bar. An invalid change leaves accepted music intact and shows its explanation locally and through Review.

Accidental changes require eligible untied single pitched notes; no control silently breaks a tie or chooses a chord tone. Chords share one duration and dot count. Their **Pitches** action opens the complete pitch-list task in Properties; enter every intended tone and use **Apply event details**. Chords support at most two differently altered pitches at the same letter/octave; a larger cluster is diagnosed rather than printed with a missing head.

For a road note, **Higher / Same / Lower**, or **Direction** on a narrow screen, changes only its relative main-pitch direction. A tied continuation stays Same. Rhythm notes and ordinary rests have written-value controls without irrelevant pitch controls. Full-measure rests display **Follows meter** instead of implying a nominal quarter note. An open slash offers **Nominal span…**, opening its staged nominal duration and dots in **Properties → Event details**. **Apply event details** accepts that span once, preserving the slash's unwritten rhythm, attached marks and source identity. Rhythmic slashes continue to use the immediate Value chooser for their prescribed attacks.

**Properties** puts attached marks first. **Event details** contains the separate staged task for event type, full pitch/chord text, full-measure-rest status and an open slash's nominal span; **Apply event details** commits its intended fields. Use the compact Pitch chooser for ordinary letter, octave and alteration corrections; full pitch text remains available here for the staged task. **Engraving** contains immediate accidental-display and stem choices. Common accepted-value controls do not read or apply unrelated pending form text. Event removal is explicit. Per-event beam membership is in Relationships and names its Properties target.

A pristine Properties view follows deliberate inspection selections in Select; it holds the inspected event while entry continues. A dirty draft retains its original target. If A has unapplied fields while B is selected, the pane identifies both rather than pretending the fields belong to B. Use **Return to …** to edit A, or **Discard and edit …** to begin B's details. B's accepted-value corrections and the fixed Write notes route remain available. Event-detail and attachment drafts can have different owners and keep distinct captions. Opening a clean form shows no repeated draft notice; conflicts and unavailable targets get their own explanation and recovery actions.

## More writing controls

**Other tools** opens three general-tool tabs in the same pane as Properties:

- **Relationships:** consecutive passage selection, ties, reviewed event conversion, and tuplets. Mixed durations and nested tuplets are supported, but a tuplet cannot cross a barline or split a nested boundary. A ratio describes written units, not the number of child notes. Ties require compatible consecutive events in one voice, potentially across bars: complete matching spelled pitch sets, rhythm notes, or valid road continuations. A tie is not a slur.
- **Instructions:** chord symbols, directions, rehearsal marks, dynamics and tempo, with musical positions and explicit recipients. These are distinct from event-owned articulations, ornaments and road intervals.
- **Measure:** clef, key, meter and beat groups, pickups, drafts, barlines, voices, explicit remainder rests, and measure movement/removal. Duplicate a bar range across the score here. Structural operations keep staff columns aligned; a meter edit affects that column and preserves later musical context.

Use Left/Right or Home/End to move focus among the three tabs, then Enter/Space to activate. Tab moves into the fields. **Back to properties** returns to the held Properties view and scroll; it is not a fourth tab. Merely selecting music does not switch the general-tool tab.

**Clear connected ties** can affect the entire connected chains touched by the selection. When that includes events outside the set, an in-UI confirmation names the expanded scope before changing it. Disjoint selection never gains intervening events to make a tie or tuplet eligible. **Per-event beam membership** is a low-level immediate marker on the event named in Properties, not a whole-group beaming command; return to that target before using it.

Unapplied forms retain their named targets through tool and view changes. Unrelated accepted edits can merge with a draft; a changed edited field or relevant musical dependency requires review. These form drafts remain available during the session but are not included in recovery or project downloads. Every accepted command is one undoable transaction; invalid operations leave the accepted score unchanged. History belongs to the session, not a revision archive in a downloaded project.

**Measure → Fill remainder with rests** previews the missing values before asking for confirmation. If all voices already fill the bar but it still carries an incomplete-draft flag, the action instead offers **Mark this full bar complete**, without adding or changing notes. Cancel preserves the draft; one Undo restores it after confirmation. A bar that is already complete adds no history.

## Instructions and parts

**Attached marks** in Properties belong to the selected event and add no written duration. Add an articulation, ornament or harmony interval, choose its type/value, then **Apply attached marks** once for the whole draft. All six articulations and five ornaments in the notation vocabulary have controls. Their engraving places them automatically opposite the drawn stem, or above when there is no stem; Author does not offer a placement override. Imported legacy placement values remain in source without controlling that automatic position. Rests and open slashes allow fermata only; ornaments require a single pitched note or road note; interval figures require a road note. The available actions reflect these constraints. Harmony intervals keep an explicit above/below direction because it changes musical meaning, not merely appearance.

Clicking a printed mark, or choosing it in the score list, retains that exact mark and its owning event without changing the writing bookmark. Passive selection opens no tools. Choose **Edit mark**, double-click its musical glyph in Select, or press Enter at the score to focus the existing row for that mark. **Remove mark** addresses that exact child, not the owning note. Removing it, selecting other music, changing parts or replacing the project clears a stale target.

For a tied road sound, each segment must explicitly contain the same interval set. To revise that harmony, choose **the complete tie chain** in Attached marks before Apply. Only harmony intervals propagate; each segment keeps its own articulations, ornaments, direction, timing and tie. The whole change takes one Undo. An equivalent final interval set leaves existing identities and source unchanged. Leaving the option off edits only the selected segment, and the validator rejects an inconsistent tied harmony without changing music. Nothing is inherited automatically.

Attachment drafts stay bound to the event and document that supplied their displayed fields. If accepted music changes, the panel shows accepted and draft values before Review can accept the conflict. An untouched externally removed row disappears during review; a locally edited missing or replaced row stays available for deliberate recovery. **Discard row change** does not delete an unrelated replacement mark. Explicit Add, Remove, Apply, Review and discard actions reveal their focused control through the tool's own scrolling containers without scrolling the score or document. Passive refresh does not move focus.

**Location & actions → Add chord symbol** opens Instructions directly at the selected staff, bar, and exact onset. **Add & next bar** or **Apply & next bar** supports a harmony pass without reopening tools; it advances only to an existing bar. Unchanged Apply advances without adding history, and an error retains the text and location. An ambiguous existing instruction requires a choice; the selector names placement and recipients and distinguishes otherwise identical items by source ID.

Positions use whole-note fractions: `0` starts the measure and `1/2` is beat three in 4/4. **At bar start** and **Use selected position** provide known positions without arithmetic. The latter captures a fixed onset; it does not attach the instruction to a moving note. Opening or editing unrelated fields preserves an imported sequential source anchor rather than silently adding an explicit position. **Open slash** leaves rhythm improvised; **Rhythmic slash** prescribes attacks while leaving pitch unspecified. Text such as “Swing” or “Solo until cue” communicates to performers; it does not create playback, repeats, or a generated accompaniment.

**Document → Score setup** adds or edits staves and groups them into named parts, including both piano staves. The current part stays visible in the header; its control opens part and location navigation from every view. Music remains shared: editing a part changes the corresponding source in the full score. Removing a part definition does not delete its musical staves.

When adding a pitched staff, **Initial clef** and **Initial key** apply to that new staff. Its later key changes follow the reference score; no instrument transposition is inferred. Rhythm and road staves omit clef and key, including any hidden values left in those fields.

The staff's **Notation** choice selects pitched five-line notation, a single-line rhythm staff, or three-roads notation. Rhythm notation hides clef/key controls and accepts rhythm notes, rests, and slashes without pitch; it still supports ordinary durations, dots, beams, tuplets, voices, and ties across bars or systems. Label the staff or add an instruction to identify the intended action or sound. Parts retain this distinction and their original source IDs.

Three roads uses Higher, Same and Lower relative to the preceding **main pitch in that voice**, including across rests. It prescribes written rhythm without fixing absolute pitch or the distance between main notes. Attached harmony figures, such as `5` above or `b3` below, each refer to that main pitch; harmony and ornament tones never become the next reference. Use **Instructions** to state the convention and scope that legend to the relevant parts. See [the three-roads grammar](three-roads.md) and [event markings](event-markings.md) for the exact vocabulary.

Changing the mode does not discard existing pitches. Add a new staff when writing another notation alongside the current music. If you deliberately want to remove pitch from an untied passage, convert its notes/chords to **rhythmic slashes**, switch the staff to **Rhythm**, and optionally convert the slashes to **rhythm notes**. Do not use open slashes if the written attacks must remain prescribed. For the reverse route, use rhythmic slashes before switching to pitched notation, then supply intended pitches explicitly. Incompatible attachments are never silently dropped: the error names the mark, staff, bar and voice, and **Review → Edit [mark]** reaches its existing row. Removing it remains your musical decision. Tied or three-roads conversions may require a complete Source edit so the staff, event kinds and ties remain valid together. See the [single-line grammar and conversion instructions](authoring.md#single-line-rhythm-staff).

Under **Who sees this instruction?**, choose the owning staff, all relevant parts, or named parts. Shared instructions can be included when extracting a part that does not contain the original staff. Review those recipients when preparing an ensemble, including the quarter-tone convention and the action intended by a rhythm line. Parts preserve absolute authored pitches: a key signature or instrument label does not transpose notes or chord text.

## Source, recovery, and files

**Source** in Write edits the full musical source, even when viewing one part. Its text remains a pending draft until **Apply source** validates and commits the whole change as one undo step. Invalid text stays available for correction; the engraving continues to show the last accepted music. **Revert to accepted score** discards the pending text. Pending Source changes block printing and musical HTML export, including draft printing, so the output cannot silently omit them.

Failed Apply shows its full explanation inside Source, associated with the text field. Closing and reopening Source, including through Review, preserves that explanation for the unchanged draft. Editing the text clears the old diagnostic but leaves the new text unapplied; Apply validates it again. Revert and project replacement clear the diagnostic along with its draft context.

A compact one-line header summary names unapplied Source, unsaved forms, or editing errors. The full message remains in its accessible label and **Review**, which routes back to the relevant source or form without applying it. An active pointer preview puts its pitch or rest value first, then action, bar and voice; an earlier refusal remains in Review rather than hiding the new proposal. Recovery and pending Source keep priority. Writing-only hints disappear in Select or Read. Routine notation notices also live in Review, so their appearance or disappearance does not move the staff; fatal rendering errors stay visible. The header does not expand the normal music palette or replace its actions.

New/Open composition, measure or part removal, passage conversion, rest filling, and short-ending approval use confirmations inside the authoring UI, with named actions and Cancel. Escape cancels the native dialog. If the music, target, view, or settings change while it is open, the old decision cannot commit; close it and review the action again. Browsers without native dialog support receive explicit in-flow controls, never an alert, prompt, or confirm fallback.

**Download project** produces versioned JSON containing musical HTML, document metadata, part definitions, instruction scopes, layout profiles, reviews, and any unapplied Source draft. **Open project or musical HTML…** accepts that JSON or supported musical HTML with one `music-staff` or `music-system` root. Scripts and unrelated page markup are rejected. **Export musical HTML** exports the currently viewed score or part in canonical notation grammar; it is not a complete project backup and does not preserve original formatting, comments, or every presentation setting.

Recovery saves are debounced after edits and stored in this browser's local storage. Web Locks coordinate cooperating Author tabs, and revision checks refuse to overwrite a newer recovery. A conflict, unavailable storage, or invalid stored data is reported rather than silently replaced. Without Web Locks, Author refuses unsafe automatic recovery writes; editing and project download remain available. Download your current copy before reloading to examine another tab's version.

A recovery warning persists in the header through navigation and routine editing feedback, with its full explanation and **Download project** action in Review. A confirmed local save clears the warning; opening a menu, correcting a note, or requesting a download does not itself prove that recovery now works. The download request also does not prove the browser saved a file. Pending Source remains in a project download and continues to guard musical editing and publication.

“Saved on this device” is not a backup, cloud sync, or confirmation of a file download. Browser data may be cleared, storage may fail, and an interrupted pending save may not finish. Keep downloaded project files for work you need to retain.

## Listen and download audio

Choose **Listen** in the view switch. **Play** prepares the currently accepted score or selected part and starts browser audio; it becomes **Pause**, then **Resume**. **Stop** returns to the beginning. Leaving Listen stops playback. A highlight follows the written event without changing the editing selection or saved writing destination.

No separate save or export is needed. Listen reads the accepted editor model, including its current octaves. Apply or discard pending Source and form edits in Write before listening. Local recovery and a downloaded project are separate from playback: recovery does not update earlier exported score/audio files. Changes to music, part, or tempo discard prepared audio, and a late render cannot start after Stop or leaving Listen.

**Starting tempo ♩ / min** scales written tempo changes proportionally. **Use score tempo** restores the written starting tempo, with 80 BPM as the fallback. These controls do not add tempo marks to the score. **Download WAV** produces 44.1 kHz mono PCM audio using the same rendered buffer as playback. Downloading does not require playing through the score. This version provides WAV rather than MP3; seeking and looping are not included.

The portable sound is a synthesized brass-like approximation, not the macOS instrument used for the original chat audio. No audio service or remote samples are needed. Notes, chords, rests, authored pitch/quarter-tones, ties, exact tuplet timing, numeric tempo marks, standard dynamic levels and basic articulations are interpreted. Staccato shortens sound without moving the next onset; tenuto holds the full written value. Text-only tempo, ornaments/fermatas and repeat signs have explicit playback notices; measures play once in written order. Rhythm, slash and three-roads events require a pitch interpretation and currently block Listen for that part. Short and empty incomplete measures play their unwritten beats as rests, keeping voices and staves aligned to the meter. This silence is included in WAV downloads; it adds no rest symbols or edits to the score. Authored pickups retain their shorter duration. Previews are bounded to ten minutes and 25,000 sounding notes.

## Prepare pages and print

Select the full score or desired part, then open **Pages**. Each has its own paper, orientation, margins, staff scale, measure numbering, maximum-measures preference, final-system justification, and boundary choices. Automatic wrapping remains the default. **Lines & page boundaries** can start a measure on a new line or page and prefer keeping it with the next measure. Explicit boundaries and actual fit take precedence over keep preferences.

Pages measures the engraving and composes complete SVG systems into paper-sized boxes with a title, running header, and page numbers. These same page elements are used for printing. This is distinct from the workbook's 680px fixed-width view, which does not compose physical pages.

Review the publication checks before printing. Musical errors and physical fit errors cannot be bypassed. Other layout warnings require inspection and acknowledgement. Incomplete measures must be completed, explicitly approved as intentional short endings where appropriate, or printed using **Print a clearly marked draft if musical work remains**. That option marks every page `DRAFT - not ready for performance`; approving a short measure does not approve an unfinished tuplet.

**Review a page turn** shows the outgoing system and the next entrance for the whole selected part. **Mark reviewed** records human review of that score and layout; relevant edits invalidate the matching review. It neither optimizes page turns nor certifies enough time to turn. A resting voice or an open improvisation instruction is not sufficient evidence of a safe turn.

Use **Print / Save as PDF** after the pages settle. In the native browser dialog, match the preview's paper size and orientation, choose **100% scale**, remove additional browser margins, and disable browser headers and footers. Author already places its chosen margins inside each page. Choose Save as PDF if the browser offers it. Printing directly before the workspace is ready is guarded against outputting stale pages.

A successful request does not prove that a dialog appeared, a PDF was saved, fonts were embedded, or a printer reproduced the preview. Inspect the actual output for page count, clipping, readable notation, and usable turns. There is no direct PDF download engine.
