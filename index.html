A notation workbook

Music Notes

Write the music in HTML. Keep its rhythm, spelling, and structure intact as it moves from a wide screen to a small one, or onto paper.

Read the markup

The checkbox fixes score wrapping at the print width; it does not show paper pages. Short scores may look unchanged. Try the automatic ensemble in a narrow window to see the difference.

Printing includes all twelve scores and their explanations, without source snippets or text transcripts. It always uses the print layout, whether the checkbox is on or off. Choose paper and margins in your browser’s dialog, then Print or Save as PDF if offered. If no dialog opens here, use a browser with printing support.

Scores follow the window width.

01

Pitch is explicit

The first bar is in G; the second changes to C. F4 always means F natural, while F#4 means F sharp. The engraving supplies the necessary cancellations.

The HTML is the authoring surface

  </music-measure>
</music-staff>

05

Ratios change time

Triplets may contain rests or mixed values. Five sixteenths in the time of four occupy one quarter. Nested ratios compose; the final bar changes to 6/8 and uses a duplet.

A triplet is a ratio, not a count of child elements 
<!-- This mixed triplet lasts one quarter-note beat. -->
<music-tuplet actual="3" normal="2">
  <music-note pitch="G5" duration="quarter"></music-note>
  <music-note pitch="E5" duration="eighth"></music-note>
</music-tuplet>
06

Room to improvise

Chord symbols and words express intent without inventing notes. Plain slashes leave the rhythm open; stemmed slashes specify rhythm without pitch. “Swing” is a performance instruction, not a hidden tuplet conversion.

07

Two hands, independent voices

The upper staff has a melody and sustained chords in separate voices. Each voice fills the measure; attacks at the same musical time align across both staves.

08

An ensemble, two layout choices

The same flute, viola, and cello passage appears twice. Only the layout instructions change. Resize the window to compare them: all three parts wrap together. Viola uses alto clef; this high cello line uses tenor clef.

Without manual interjection

Automatic wrapping is the default. No line breaks, page breaks, keep hints, or measure limits are authored here. The opening pickup stays with bar 1 when they fit together; the remaining measures wrap as needed. Clefs repeat at new systems, and authored ties continue across any breaks.

Automatic layout needs no break attributes 
<!-- Each part keeps its musical pickup, ties, rests, and barlines. -->
<music-measure meter="3/4" pickup number="0">…</music-measure>
<music-measure>…</music-measure>
<music-measure>…</music-measure>
<music-measure end-bar="final">…</music-measure>

With manual interjection

Author-directed breaks are optional overrides. The pickup asks to stay with bar 1. Bar 2 starts a new line, and bar 3 requests a new printed page after the rests. Those breaks remain even when a wider window could fit more music. Narrow widths can add further lines.

The same measures, with optional layout instructions 
<music-measure meter="3/4" pickup number="0" keep-with-next>…</music-measure>
<music-measure>…</music-measure>
<music-measure break-before="line">…</music-measure>
<music-measure break-before="page" end-bar="final">…</music-measure>

Both versions use the same 680px print width. Use print layout on screen fixes the system layout; it does not show physical paper pages. Without a requested page break, the browser handles ordinary pagination. The manual page marker illustrates an author choice, not an automatically calculated safe turn. Review the final pages with your paper settings and playing tempo.

09

Quarter-tones and a clapped pulse

The melody uses Stein–Zimmermann signs in 24 equal divisions of the octave: quarter-sharp/flat mean ±50 cents from the natural, and three-quarter-sharp/flat mean ±150 cents. The lower staff has one line and no pitch; clap its written attacks. Both parts share 7/8 grouped 2 + 2 + 3.

The second rhythm bar includes a quarter note and an eighth rest inside 3:2: two written events span three eighth-note units, scaled to one quarter. The final rest fills all seven eighths. The part labels and brief instructions belong to their own staves, so each remains meaningful when extracted.

10

3 roads music

Choose a comfortable reference pitch before the first attack. The top road means choose any higher pitch, the middle means the same pitch, and the bottom means any lower pitch. Compare each new attack with the last main pitch in that voice; rests keep that reference. The rhythm is written, but the movement between main pitches has no fixed interval, register, or absolute pitch.

Only the top, middle, and bottom lines of a normal five-line staff remain, at their original spacing. Slash heads prescribe the rhythm, as in “Room to improvise.” Two top-road attacks each move higher; an untied middle-road slash repeats the previous main pitch. The tied middle-road continuation in the last bar holds the pitch without another attack.

Directions carry meaning without fixing pitches 
<music-staff notation="three-roads" meter="4/4">
  <music-measure>
    <music-road direction="same" duration="quarter"></music-road>
    <music-road direction="higher" duration="eighth"></music-road>
    <music-road direction="higher" duration="eighth"></music-road>
    <music-road direction="same" duration="quarter"></music-road>
    <music-road direction="lower" duration="quarter"></music-road>
  </music-measure>
</music-staff>
11

Mark the attack; add a harmony

Articulations and ornaments sit opposite the printed stem, or above when there is no stem. Staccato changes how a note is released, not its written duration. On the three-roads staff, each interval figure adds a harmony above or below that event’s main pitch. Harmony notes and ornament auxiliaries never change the next road’s main-pitch reference.

One possible realization of the first two road events: choose F as the first main pitch and 5 above adds C above; choose Bb for the next main pitch and b3 below adds G below. These pitches explain the interval rule; they are not stored as road pitches. Compound intervals retain their size through 13. The final tie holds the same complete harmony; both ends explicitly carry the same interval set.

Markings are children of their musical event 
<music-note pitch="F4" duration="quarter">
  <music-articulation type="staccato"></music-articulation>
  <music-ornament type="trill"></music-ornament>
</music-note>

<music-road direction="same" duration="half">
  <music-interval value="5" placement="above"></music-interval>
  <music-interval value="b3" placement="below"></music-interval>
</music-road>
